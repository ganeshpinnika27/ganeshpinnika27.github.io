Research / Plant genomics

De Novo Plastome Assembly and Comparative Genomics of Elatostema lineolatum var. major

Rana, M.S., Pinnika, G., Chetri, B.K. (2025). Biology Bulletin Reviews 15, 810-821. Second author.

My role: Performed the ortholog analysis of the plastome sequences. (from the paper's author contributions)

Read the paper

Summary

Elatostema lineolatum var. major is a wild edible and medicinal plant from Bhutan with no prior genomic resources. This study assembled its first complete chloroplast genome (plastome) and compared it with 242 plastomes from the nettle family (Urticaceae) to study genome structure, selection, candidate DNA barcodes and phylogenetic placement.

Key result. A complete 150,292 bp plastome with the typical four-part structure and 131 genes. Across Urticaceae, plastome structure is highly conserved; ycf1 emerged as a promising DNA barcode, and ycf2 showed signs of positive selection.

Approach

  • Sequencing and assembly: Illumina NovaSeq sequencing and de novo assembly with NOVOPlasty (rbcL seed), annotated with GeSeq and curated against related Elatostema plastomes.
  • Comparative genomics: 242 Urticaceae plastomes from NCBI; inverted repeat boundaries, nucleotide diversity (DnaSP) and simple sequence repeats.
  • Selection and codon usage: Ka/Ks ratios per gene and codon usage bias analysis.
  • Orthology and phylogeny: OrthoFinder on 18 plastomes, and a maximum likelihood tree of 73 plastomes with 5,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates.

  • Gene order and synteny are highly conserved among Elatostema species, supporting the genus as a natural group.
  • Nucleotide diversity analysis identified eleven hypervariable regions; ycf1 is the strongest barcode candidate.
  • Most genes are under purifying selection, while Ka/Ks above 1 in genes including ycf2 suggests adaptive evolution.

Takeaways

  • The first genomic resource for a culturally important wild edible plant of Bhutan.
  • Candidate barcodes and SSR markers for species identification and conservation genetics.
