Genomic Characterization of a MAGIC Population Containing Introgressions from Four Tetraploid Gossypium Species

Pinnika, G., Li, P., McCarty, J.C., Jenkins, J.N., Fang, D.D. (2026). The Plant Genome 19(4), e70308. First author.

Read the paper

Summary

Wild and related tetraploid cotton species carry useful alleles, but crossing whole genomes into upland cotton brings unwanted effects. Chromosome substitution lines (CSLs) replace one chromosome at a time with a donor chromosome from G. barbadense, G. mustelinum or G. tomentosum. Crossing 32 CSLs with five upland cultivars produced a multiparent advanced generation intercross (MAGIC) population of 797 lines. This study sequenced every parent and line to measure how much donor DNA each one actually carries.

Key result. 794 of 797 MAGIC lines carry donor-species segments, on average on nine chromosomes, and large segments persisted after five rounds of random mating, showing how hard linkage drag is to break in interspecific breeding.

Results

FindingResult
CSLs with the targeted chromosome substitution28 of 32
Residual non-target introgressions in CSLsPresent in all lines
SNPs across the 797 MAGIC RILs7,333,111 (4.78 million A subgenome, 2.55 million D subgenome)
RILs carrying introgressions794 of 797, on an average of 9 chromosomes
RILs dominated by G. barbadense segments356
Population structureFour kinship groups

The predominance of G. barbadense segments suggests its alleles outcompeted those of the other donors during population development. Each MAGIC line still derives over 95% of its genome from upland cotton, so the introgressions are targeted rather than genome-wide.

Takeaways

  • Whole-genome sequencing of every line is needed to know what a chromosome substitution or MAGIC line really contains.
  • The population and its genotype data are a resource for mapping traits contributed by wild species.
